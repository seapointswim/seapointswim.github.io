Tide height is the number on a tide table. Water depth at the steps varies with the seabed. We use the Marine Institute’s Seapoint Beach tide curve and estimate heights on the Dublin tide-table scale. This makes the heights and times approximate.

Wind, gusts, air temperature and rain come from Open-Meteo’s nearest forecast grid cell to Seapoint Bathing Place beside the steps. Wind is modelled 10 m above the surface. Shelter and gusts at the steps can differ.

The planner rules out sustained winds from about 11.3 m/s, onshore winds from 9.8 m/s or gusts from 14.9 m/s. These are rounded planning cut-offs. Met Éireann classifies 11.3 m/s as a strong breeze.

Water below 15°C brings cold-water shock risk (RNLI). The wind-chill index estimates how cold exposed, dry skin feels in moving air, not wet-skin cooling after a swim. Sea temperatures are forecast at a nearby model grid cell, not measured at Seapoint. Any M2 buoy reading is offshore.

An old routine EPA water sample does not automatically reduce the recommendation. Active warnings/restrictions matter. The buoy is offshore; its waves are not necessarily those at Seapoint. Check ferry wash and exit conditions.
