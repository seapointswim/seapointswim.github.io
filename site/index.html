The Marine Institute models tides for Seapoint Beach at a point about 300 metres offshore. Heights are relative to the model’s mean sea level. Water depth at the steps depends on the seabed. The approximate ±3-hour rule around high water varies with the height of high tide; it is not a validated swimming threshold.

The planner excludes periods with sustained winds of 22 kn or more, onshore winds of 19 kn or more, or gusts of 29 kn or more. These are planning cut-offs. Met Éireann classifies 22 kn as a strong breeze.

Water below 15°C brings cold-water shock risk (RNLI). The wind-chill index estimates how cold exposed, dry skin feels in moving air, not wet-skin cooling after a swim. Sea temperatures are forecast at a nearby model grid cell, not measured at Seapoint. Any M2 buoy reading is offshore.

An old routine EPA water sample does not automatically reduce the recommendation. Active warnings/restrictions matter. The buoy is offshore; its waves are not necessarily those at Seapoint. Check ferry wash and exit conditions.
